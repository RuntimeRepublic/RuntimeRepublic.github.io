The Pastor Joins

Five Weeks on the Team · Episode 3 of 10 · about 7 minutes · Draft under review

Karen Reyes did not plan to tell anyone. It came out on a Tuesday afternoon in the church office, where she has managed the front desk for eleven years. Pastor Dan had stopped by her desk to ask about the bulletin, and he noticed she had been crying. He closed the door and sat down, and after a long minute Karen said the words out loud for the first time. She and Tom were not doing well. They had not talked about money at the dinner table in months. Some nights they did not talk at all.

Pastor Dan has sat with a lot of couples in that office. He asked the question he always asks first: would they both come in? Karen said she would ask Tom. That night she did, and to her surprise, Tom said yes.

They came in on a Thursday evening. Pastor Dan expected the usual: two people, two stories, and a long silence between them. Instead, ten minutes in, Tom said something he had not heard before. "The only place we still agree is the app." Karen nodded. Pastor Dan asked what app.

So they showed him. Tom opened the team app on his phone, and Karen opened hers. They showed him the weekly close: every dollar in and out, labeled and checked, nothing guessed. They showed him the team packet they sent every Sunday, with call signs and percentages and no names. And they showed him the part that had been quietly holding them together. Each Saturday, when the family closed the week, they each wrote a short note. Tom's notes and Karen's notes did not agree about the budget. But both of them kept writing, and both of them kept reading the other's note.

Pastor Dan asked to see the notes. They hesitated, then turned the phones toward him. Tom had written about the tuition bill and the fear that they would never retire. Karen had written about Ava's band fees and the feeling that she was always the one who said no. Reading them side by side, Pastor Dan saw what neither of them could see from inside their own note. They were not fighting about money. They were each afraid, about the same three children, in different words.

He asked a question that changed the evening. "What do you two want? Not this week. In ten years."

Neither of them had an answer ready. The app had shown them where every dollar went, but it had never asked them where they wanted to go.

Pastor Dan asked if he could join their team. He was curious, and he was honest about why. He meets with couples every week, and almost every one of them is fighting about money underneath whatever else they are fighting about. Tom sent him an invite that night. Pastor Dan joined on his phone, set up his own call sign, and closed his first week that Saturday like everyone else. He found his own pay stub as confusing as Luke had found his.

The next week, the three of them met again, and Pastor Dan brought an idea. The big team had seven households now: Maria, Dale, Ruth, the Lewises, the Reyes family, and a new member or two. That was the right place for weekly packets and civic work. But what Tom and Karen needed was smaller and more private. So the three of them started a private team of three, just for the couple and their pastor. No packets went to anyone else. Its first job was not taxes or contact letters. It was the question Pastor Dan had asked: what do you two want, together?

That was the night the app grew a new tab. They called it Life Goals.

The Life Goals tab works the way everything else in the app works: one question at a time, with choices to tap and room to write your own. It started by asking Tom and Karen, separately, to name what mattered most to them in ten years. Then it showed both answers side by side, the same way it shows today's paycheck beside the replacement plan. To their surprise, the lists overlapped more than they disagreed. Both of them wrote that they wanted all three children through school without debt. Both of them wanted to stay in their house. Both of them wanted, someday, to stop working at sixty-five and take Ava to see the ocean. Karen cried again when she read Tom's list, but this time for a different reason.

Then the tab did what the app always does. It connected the goals to the numbers. Each goal got a line in the weekly budget, a target, and a check every Saturday. Sam's tuition became a goal with a monthly amount and a trail to follow. Retirement became a goal with a date. The ocean trip became a small goal with its own savings line, and somehow that one mattered most. Every Saturday, the weekly close now ended with the Life Goals tab: are we moving toward what we said we wanted, or away from it?

Pastor Dan did not run the meetings. He asked questions, the way the app does, and he made sure both of them answered. When Tom and Karen disagreed about a goal, the tab did what the team workshop does with every hard problem. It asked each of them for their own idea first and wrote it down as theirs. Then the three of them weighed both ideas together. Some weeks Tom's idea won. Some weeks Karen's did. A few times they found a third idea better than both.

Five weeks into their private team, Tom and Karen are still married. Their story is not finished, and nobody on the team pretends it is. There will be another episode, and it may be a hard one. Some marriages heal and some do not. But whatever happens next, they will face it with the same numbers in front of both of them, the same goals written in both their words, and a pastor who knows exactly what they are carrying.

On the last Sunday of the five weeks, Pastor Dan posted one short note in the private team thread. He wrote that he had counseled couples for twenty years, and he had never before had a tool that showed both people the same truth at the same time. Then he asked whether the app could help him start private teams for the other couples he meets with. Tom answered first. He said yes, and that he would help set them up.
